## Known overlap

Hlawka's equidistribution and Fujii's discrepancy are prior results stated
in Ford-Zaharescu's research paper. FZ and Ford-Soundararajan-Zaharescu already
study circle-valued zero ordinates and prime-resonant secondary terms. Our
quotient theorem must not be presented as discovering those phenomena.
BCY supplies the positive simple-critical-line proportion. Support exclusion,
residue packing and closure mass are corollaries of those inputs.

Even real entire factors and prescribed zeros are standard product tools.
Functions sharing Riemann's functional equation without being ordinary
Dirichlet series are also established literature. Kaczorowski-Molteni-Perelli
(2010), Introduction p. 1, states the precise Hamburger converse theorem.
Nakamura (2026), DOI 10.1007/s10231-026-01726-9, Introduction 1.2, discusses
related non-Dirichlet constructions and explicitly locates the Hamburger
boundary. The present real-axis obstruction is proved directly and does not
require invoking a converse theorem.

The defensible theme is therefore a synthesis and obstruction study: a
quantitative cylinder/Mobius observation theorem, followed by an explicit
infinite sparse perturbation preserving the named coarse and secondary
asymptotic classes, and a concrete arithmetic condition that rejects it.
This combination may be useful without a claim of first discovery.
