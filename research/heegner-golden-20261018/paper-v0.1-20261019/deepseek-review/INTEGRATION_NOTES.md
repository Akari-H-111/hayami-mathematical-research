# Integration notes: a coherent initial-paper narrative

**Scope.** These notes recommend how to assemble the reviewed pieces into an initial paper. They are based on the five supplied packets and the checks in `ADVICE_REVIEW.md`; they do not certify the external CM/ray-class theorems, the conductor computations, or the unseen manuscript. The aim is a coherent, bounded paper, not a novelty verdict.

## Recommended narrative

### 1. Opening: Hermite and the pentagonal discriminant

Open with one short paragraph on Hermite’s near-integer observation,
\[
e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}\approx 640320^3+744,
\]
as the historical entry point. Immediately state the boundary: the paper does **not** estimate the exponential error. Hermite supplies the historical question, not the method.

Then introduce the ruled-surface rank-loss quadratic
\[
p(c)=c^2-3c+1=(c-\varphi^2)(c-\varphi^{-2}),
\qquad \operatorname{disc}(p)=5,
\]
with the rank-loss root
\[
c_0=\varphi^{-2}=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}=\gamma^2,
\qquad \gamma=\varphi^{-1}.
\]
State the field identity
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)^+
=\mathbb Q(2\cos(2\pi/5)).
\]
Add the shared-field caution in one sentence: the surface parameter and the level-\(5\) cyclotomic setting share a quadratic field, but this is not a map between the surface and a modular curve. The paper’s question is whether the same scalar parameter can be recovered from an actual level-\(5\) CM value, and what Fourier structure the level-\(5\) coordinate carries. Use “relation” or “common field,” not “equivalence.”

### 2. The \(\tau_{163}\) arithmetic anchor

Place the \(\tau_{163}\) reconstruction early, before the Fourier story, so the arithmetic content is visible. Define
\[
\tau_{163}=\frac{1+i\sqrt{163}}2,\qquad
q=e^{2\pi i\tau_{163}}=-e^{-\pi\sqrt{163}},\qquad
t=r(\tau_{163})^5<0.
\]
Define the level-\(5\) Rogers–Ramanujan product \(r\) once here, or refer explicitly to its definition in Section 3; do not leave \(t=r(\tau_{163})^5\) undefined. State the fifth-root phase convention explicitly: \(q^{1/5}=e^{2\pi i\tau/5}\), equivalently \(r=e^{\pi i/5}s\) and \(t=-s^5\) with \(s>0\). The sign certificate is for this branch. State the reconstruction as a conditional theorem:

> With the explicit integer polynomials \(A,B\) from the CM certificate,
> \[
> P_{163}(t)=A(t)^2-5B(t)^2,\qquad \gcd(A,B)=1.
> \]
> On the branch \(-5\cdot10^{-18}<t<-10^{-18}\), one has \(A(t)<0<B(t)\), hence \(A(t)/B(t)=-\sqrt5\), and therefore
> \[
> \varphi=\frac{B-A}{2B},\qquad
> \gamma=-\frac{A+B}{2B},\qquad
> c_0=\frac{3B+A}{2B}.
> \]
> Consequently \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\subseteq\mathbb Q(t)\) and the surface’s \(c_0\) is recovered from the CM value \(t\).

The integers \(a,\dots,f\) in \(A,B\) are displayed in `CM_BASELINE.md` §3; put them in a paper appendix or a small table, not in a footnote. Then separate the layers explicitly:

| Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| \(A^2-5B^2=P_{163}\), \(\gcd(A,B)=1\) | Local exact algebra; independently confirmed. |
| Sign enclosure and Sturm count | Local rational certificate; exact, but the paper should include the enclosure or cite the verification appendix. |
| Klein’s level-\(5\) equation | Invoked classical input; cite it, do not reprove it. |
| \(j(\tau_{163})=-640320^3\) | Invoked classical CM value; cite it, do not present it as new. |
| Quartic embedding and conductor \(815\) | External class-field input and a known cyclic-quartic family; optional, not the main theorem. |

The reconstruction is the cleanest \(\tau_{163}\) insertion: minimal classical input, exact local algebra, and a direct route to \(c_0\). The sign certificate is essential, not cosmetic: the positive sign would recover \(\gamma\) and \(\varphi^2\) instead of \(\varphi\) and \(c_0\).

**Optional quartic remark.** If space permits, add a short remark that the same singular-value field contains the cyclic quartic generated by
\[
\delta=2\sqrt{163}\sin\frac{\pi}{5},
\qquad
\varphi\delta=2\sqrt{163}\sin\frac{2\pi}{5},
\]
with conductor \(815\) and discriminant \(3321125\), and that \(c_0\) is the ratio of the squares of the two positive conjugates. Label this as a specialization of known cyclic-quartic/class-field theory, cite the external sources, and do not claim a new CM theorem. The quartic is stronger structural information, but it is heavier and invites novelty claims the packet does not support. The initial paper can omit it.

### 3. The level-\(5\) coordinate at \(\tau=i\) and \(A=JMJ\)

Now introduce the level-\(5\) Rogers–Ramanujan coordinate
\[
r(\tau)=q^{1/5}\prod_{n\ge1}
\frac{(1-q^{5n-4})(1-q^{5n-1})}
{(1-q^{5n-3})(1-q^{5n-2})},
\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau},
\]
and Duke’s inversion law
\[
r(-1/\tau)=\frac{\gamma-r(\tau)}{1+\gamma r(\tau)}.
\]
At \(\tau=i\), the inversion is a fixed-point equation; positivity of the product gives the positive root
\[
\kappa=r(i)=\frac{\gamma}{1+\sqrt{1+c_0}},
\qquad
0<\kappa<1.
\]
Define
\[
M=\frac1{\sqrt{1+c_0}}
\begin{pmatrix}-1&\gamma\\ \gamma&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=JMJ.
\]
Then \(M^2=I\) and \(A\) is the reflection
\[
A=\frac1d\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma\\ \gamma&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad d=\sqrt{1+c_0}.
\]
With \(\theta=\arctan\kappa\),
\[
\kappa=\tan\theta,\qquad
\gamma=\tan(2\theta)=\frac{2\kappa}{1-\kappa^2},
\qquad
u_0=\frac{\gamma}{2}=\frac12\tan(2\theta).
\]
The \(+1\) eigenline of \(A\) is \(\mathbb R(1,\kappa)\), at angle \(\theta\); the \(-1\) eigenline is \(\mathbb R(1,-1/\kappa)\). State explicitly that \(\theta\approx15.8587^\circ\), not \(18^\circ\). The exact form \(\kappa=2\cos(\pi/10)-2\cos(\pi/5)\) is a useful check.

Keep the roles distinct: \(\tau=i\) selects the \(+1\) eigenline through the fixed point of the level-\(5\) inversion; \(\tau_{163}\) supplies the arithmetic field reconstruction. They are both level-\(5\) CM values, but they answer different questions. Do not merge them into a single “golden Heegner” claim.

The norm quartic
\[
\kappa^4+2\kappa^3-6\kappa^2-2\kappa+1
=N_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)(X)/\mathbb Q(X)}(X^2+2\varphi X-1)\big|_{X=\kappa}
\]
belongs here as a short elementary remark, not as a headline theorem.

### 4. The Fourier involution and the Davenport–Heilbronn functional equation

Introduce the real odd \(5\)-periodic space
\[
V=\{a:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb R\mid a_{n+5}=a_n,\ a_{-n}=-a_n\}
\simeq\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
a=(x,y,-y,-x,0),
\]
the normalized positive-exponential DFT
\[
(Fa)_m=\frac1{\sqrt5}\sum_{n=1}^5a_ne^{2\pi imn/5},
\qquad
T=-iF.
\]
Then \(T^2=I\) on \(V\), and in the \((x,y)\) coordinates \(T\) is represented by the reflection \(A\) above. The two eigenlines are \(\mathbb R(1,\kappa)\) and \(\mathbb R(1,-1/\kappa)\). The \(+1\) sequence is
\[
a=(1,\kappa,-\kappa,-1,0).
\]
Define
\[
\Theta_a(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}na_ne^{-\pi n^2t/5},
\qquad
\Lambda_a(s)=\frac12\int_0^\infty\Theta_a(t)t^{(s+1)/2}\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
The theta transformation and Mellin integral give
\[
\Theta_a(t)=t^{-3/2}\Theta_{Ta}(1/t),
\qquad
\Lambda_a(s)=\Lambda_{Ta}(1-s).
\]
State the classification theorem:

> For \(a\in V\) and \(\varepsilon=\pm1\),
> \[
> \Lambda_a(s)=\varepsilon\Lambda_a(1-s)\ \text{for all }s
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> Ta=\varepsilon a.
> \]
> The proof needs the injectivity of \(a\mapsto\Lambda_a\): if \(\Lambda_a\equiv0\), then \(F_a\equiv0\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\); the least nonzero coefficient contradicts the limit \(\sigma\to+\infty\). The classification is complete inside the two-dimensional space \(V\): the \(+1\) equation holds exactly on \(\mathbb R(1,\kappa)\), the \(-1\) equation exactly on \(\mathbb R(1,-1/\kappa)\), and the zero vector satisfies both.

Then present the Davenport–Heilbronn sequence as the \(+1\) example:
\[
a=(1,\kappa,-\kappa,-1,0),
\qquad
\Lambda_a(s)=\Lambda_a(1-s).
\]
Note that it is nonmultiplicative (\(a_6=1\) while \(a_2a_3=-\kappa^2\)) and that classical Davenport–Heilbronn theory has zeros off \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). This is the right place for the sentence that functional-equation symmetry alone does not fix zero locations. Do not claim the classification covers all Dirichlet series, and do not mention RH except to say it is not addressed.

If space permits, the odd-\(N\) appendix goes after this section. It should state the normalized sine-Fourier involution and the same Poisson/Mellin mechanism with \(N\) in place of \(5\), and explicitly disclaim any \(N\)-dependent ruled-surface family or canonical \(\kappa_N\). Keep it bounded; it is a Fourier extension, not a universal arithmetic theory.

### 5. Synthesis and boundaries

End with a short synthesis that separates three layers:

1. **Elementary pentagonal field.** \(p(c)\), discriminant \(5\), \(c_0=\varphi^{-2}\), and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)^+\).
2. **Conditional \(\tau_{163}\) reconstruction.** The exact identity \(A^2-5B^2=P_{163}\), the rational sign certificate, and the formulas for \(\varphi,\gamma,c_0\). This is a scalar field inclusion, conditional on classical modular inputs.
3. **Fourier/Mellin classification.** The involution \(T\), the reflection \(A=JMJ\), and the equivalence between \(Ta=\varepsilon a\) and the completed functional equation inside \(V\).

State the open boundaries plainly: no geometric/operator/cohomological map between the CM fibre and the ruled surface is constructed; no canonical mark carrying the quadratic character is constructed; no estimate or explanation of the near-integer error is proved; no \(N\)-dependent ruled-surface family is constructed. The common field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) is a meeting point, not a global equivalence.

### Suggested abstract

> We study a pentagonal rank-loss parameter \(c_0=\varphi^{-2}\) of a ruled surface and its relation to the level-\(5\) Rogers–Ramanujan coordinate. The parameter lies in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)^+\), and the same scalar \(c_0\) is recovered from the CM value \(t=r(\tau_{163})^5\) by an explicit branch-certified identity \(A^2-5B^2=P_{163}\), conditional on the classical level-\(5\) modular equation and \(j(\tau_{163})=-640320^3\). At \(\tau=i\) the level-\(5\) inversion selects a positive fixed value \(\kappa=\tan\theta\), and the normalized Fourier involution on real odd \(5\)-periodic sequences is represented by the reflection \(A=JMJ\) with \(+1\) eigenline \((1,\kappa)\). The associated theta/Mellin completion satisfies a functional equation \(\Lambda_a(s)=\varepsilon\Lambda_a(1-s)\) exactly when \(Ta=\varepsilon a\); this classifies the two-dimensional coefficient space and yields the Davenport–Heilbronn functional equation as the \(+1\) case. The \(\tau_{163}\) recovery is a scalar field statement, not a geometric identification, and no new claim about zero locations or the near-integer error is made.

## What to cut or relocate

- **Keep in the main paper:** the pentagonal field lemma, the shared-field caution, the half-angle/reflection lemma, the norm quartic as a short remark, the Fourier involution and classification theorem, and the conditional \(\tau_{163}\) reconstruction.
- **Keep as a brief remark or appendix:** the quartic embedding, conductor \(815\), discriminant \(3321125\), and the pentagon-length interpretation. These are structurally interesting but heavier and prior-art-sensitive.
- **Relocate or omit:** the \(166\)-type splitting table, the Artin-law catalogue, practical cryptography, BigBridge/RH speculation, and any claim that the golden field explains the near-integer error. These do not belong in the initial paper’s main narrative.
- **Compress:** the thirteen-control CM table and the \(j=1,2\) irreducibility controls. One sentence each is enough to make the point that the sextic quotient is universal but golden inclusion is not automatic.
- **Do not use:** the \(j_{-15}\) shared-field identity as the \(\tau_{163}\) bridge. It is a control against mistaking affine field overlap for an intrinsic map.

## Suggested title and framing

A title that names the three objects is safer than a title that asserts a unified theory. For example:

> *A pentagonal rank-loss coordinate, a Fourier involution, and a conditional field recovery at \(-163\)*

or

> *From a pentagonal ruled surface to a Davenport–Heilbronn functional equation*

Avoid titles such as “A golden Heegner theorem” or “A new CM theory of the ruled surface.” The paper’s claim is a coherent conditional link and a complete classification inside a two-dimensional coefficient space, not a new general CM theorem.

## Bottom line

The recommended order is:

1. Hermite and the discriminant-\(5\) pentagonal field.
2. The branch-certified \(\tau_{163}\) reconstruction of \(c_0\).
3. The \(\tau=i\) level-\(5\) coordinate, \(\kappa=\tan\theta\), and \(A=JMJ\).
4. The Fourier/Mellin involution and the Davenport–Heilbronn functional equation.
5. Boundaries and open questions.

This order follows the mandate’s arc from Hermite/discriminant \(5\) to the actual \(\tau_{163}\) recovery to \(A=JMJ\) and DH. It keeps the arithmetic anchor before the structural Fourier story, so the reader sees why the level-\(5\) coordinate matters before encountering the involution. The paper remains honest about what is elementary, what is classical, what is conditional, and what is not constructed. No forced novelty verdict is needed; the claim boundaries do the work.
