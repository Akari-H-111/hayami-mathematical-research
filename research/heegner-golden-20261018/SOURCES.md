## Classical and modern external sources

1. William Duke, *Continued Fractions and Modular Functions*, Bulletin of the
   AMS 42 (2005), 137--162.  Author PDF:
   <https://www.math.ucla.edu/~wdduke/preprints/bams4.pdf>; DOI:
   <https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-05-01047-5>.

   Used for the standard (r(\tau)) convention, the icosahedral equation
   (2.5), the modular interpretation, and the CM/radical context. The worker's
   direct fetch timed out / failed sandbox DNS, as retained in the CLI log.
   The supervising host subsequently retrieved the complete 31-page author
   PDF to `logs/sources/duke-bams4.pdf` and extracted searchable text beside it.
   The retrieval hash is recorded in `logs/sources/RETRIEVAL.json`. Availability
   of the full text is not a claim that every argument in it was audited.
   In particular, Theorem 3 is about the standard r(tau) at algebraic tau in
   the upper half-plane: its only values of degree at most two over Q are
   plus/minus i. It is not a statement about the phase-stripped s-branch.  The supplied lead additionally identifies its Theorem 3 as the
   stronger degree-at-most-two CM exclusion; this package does not try to
   rederive that result.

2. Rajeev Kohli, *Properties of reciprocity formulas for the
   Rogers--Ramanujan continued fractions*, arXiv:1806.05739v7 (2019),
   <https://arxiv.org/html/1806.05739v7>.

   Retrieved in full HTML.  Equation (29) gives
   
   \[
   R(e^{-2\alpha})=\frac{1-\phi R(e^{-2\beta})}{\phi+R(e^{-2\beta})},
   \quad\alpha\beta=\pi^2,
   \]

   and the fixed point (R(e^{-2\pi})=\sqrt{\phi^2+1}-\phi).  These
   golden-ratio reciprocity facts are classical/previously discussed, not new
   claims here.

3. Bryden Cais and Brian Conrad, *Modular Curves and Ramanujan's Continued
   Fraction*, <https://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/J5final.pdf>.

   Retrieved as a 62-page PDF.  Corollary 5.7 describes the abelian extension
   generated by their level-5 coordinate at a CM point, its ramification, and
   ray-class-field equality under stated integrality/unit hypotheses.  This is
   the appropriate known framework for a future stabilizer explanation.  It
   does not by itself prove the exact $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$-subfield pattern
   established by this finite calculation.

4. William Duke, *Modular functions and the uniform distribution of CM
   points*, author PDF: <https://math.ucla.edu/~wdduke/preprints/modud.pdf>.

   The first page of the recovered full author text gives the singular-modulus
   table, including
   
   \[
   j\!\left(\frac{-1+i\sqrt{15}}2\right)
   =\frac{-191025-85995\sqrt5}{2}
   \]

   and the discriminant (-20) value. The supervising host subsequently
   retrieved the full author PDF to `logs/sources/duke-modud.pdf` and extracted
   searchable text beside it. Its hash is recorded in the same retrieval JSON.
   Only the relevant passages, rather than the entire paper, are relied on.

5. John M. Campbell, *Applications of the icosahedral equation for the
   Rogers--Ramanujan continued fraction*, arXiv:2402.02546v1,
   <https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02546v1>.

   Retrieved in full HTML.  It confirms the product convention and documents
   contemporary use of the icosahedral equation for exact CM-style evaluations.

6. Bruce C. Berndt and Örs Rebák, *The Rogers--Ramanujan continued fraction*,
   arXiv:2512.19952v1, <https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19952v1>.

   Retrieved in full HTML as a recent survey/context source.  It is not needed
   for the algebraic proof in this package.

## Literature-position conclusion

The source comparison supports a conservative conclusion.  Level-5
Rogers--Ramanujan singular values, their icosahedral equations, and CM/ray
class fields are established theory.  This package has not located a source
that names the particular (-163) (A^2-5B^2) factorization or the full
13-control pattern as a standalone theorem.  That absence is not evidence of
novelty.  No novelty claim is made until a specific Shimura-reciprocity
stabilizer calculation and a fuller literature review are completed.

## Additional director source comparison

Sumit Kumar Jha, *Cyclic Kronecker Fourier Transforms, Weber Norms, and
Rogers-Ramanujan Singular Values*, arXiv:2610.18910v1, submitted 12 October
2026, <https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18910>. The director retrieved the full HTML
and inspected the standing hypotheses and stated field-degree results. It
concerns fundamental D=-5n, ramified 5, and class group C_(4M), unlike the
-163 inert-5 / conductor-5 lead. Its genus-field descent is related prior
work, not a theorem independently verified or applied here.

Michel Waldschmidt, *Transcendence of Periods*, 2011 lecture,
<https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michel.waldschmidt/articles/pdf/PeriodsVI.pdf>,
discusses Gelfond-Schneider and exp(pi sqrt(d)), including 163. Taking the
branch (-1)^(-i sqrt(163)) shows that exp(pi sqrt(163)) is transcendental.
It therefore cannot equal an expression algebraic over Q(phi); the proved
connection here passes through a modular-function special value instead.
