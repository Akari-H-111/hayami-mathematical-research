## Portrait and landscape policy

- **Article:** portrait throughout. Use ordinary `figure` environments with flexible placement, typically top or bottom, and preserve the nearby paragraph/theorem unit. Replace compulsory `\Plate` page breaks in the article mechanism. Do not force `[H]` placement as a substitute for solving size and order.
- **Atlas:** portrait by default. Permit a landscape sheet only for a specific wide multi-panel comparison that is demonstrably more legible that way. A wide current asset or a large figure budget alone is insufficient justification. Group any necessary landscape sheets so readers do not repeatedly change viewing mode.
- Rebuild selected graphics at intended physical size. Preserve aspect ratio of all supplied scientific content; equal-axis plots must remain equal-axis. Remove internal figure numbers, repeated color-key footers, and duplicate slide-style titles from body assets. Keep essential mathematical labels and transfer explanatory prose to captions.
- Caption each figure with its object, claim or illustration purpose, normalization, and necessary domain. Put full numerical methods in atlas notes. Mark diagnostics as numerical. Use panel letters consistently and regenerate every affected cross-reference and text-layer title.

## Acceptance checks for an implementation

1. **Content preservation:** map every original Figure 1–12 to a body group or atlas location. Confirm that vertices, chord counts, domains, conventions, exact ratios, and computational explanations survive where relevant. Check that no proof or claim changes as a byproduct of moving figures.
2. **Result hierarchy:** first two pages state what is proved and which external inputs are used. The two CM arguments have explicit distinct roles. The scope of Corollary A.1 is apparent if retained as a principal result.
3. **Continuity:** no figure sequence divides a proof, no return begins with a contextless “Also,” and Theorems 4.1–4.2 are read as one development. A figure is introduced and discussed near its first use. No forced blank lower half is retained merely to begin a plate.
4. **Visual QA:** render and inspect every page of both proposed outputs at ordinary page-view scale and at final print size. Check clipped labels, long formulas, small legends, thin lines, crowded captions, large 3D dead areas, footer collisions, and awkward section breaks. Judge whitespace by the separation or grouping it achieves.
5. **Typography:** target 9–10 pt in body graphics; review any essential label below 8 pt. Do not use smaller type to hit a page estimate. Ensure color encodings survive grayscale through line style, symbols, or labels. Confirm geometric circles and lengths retain their intended scale.
6. **Numerical captions:** Figures 7, 9–12 and the rank-loss heatmap carry the relevant precision/truncation/grid/residual or floor information. Scalar theta/functional-equation plots name the eigenvector. Plot notation matches the body. Numerical claims await actual replay rather than acceptance from appearance.
7. **Technical export:** check page boxes, rotation, figure numbering, accessible/copied text, bookmarks, links, citations, and all body/atlas cross-references. Hidden asset numbering must not conflict with visible captions. Confirm the output compiles using the actual authorized asset set; this review did not compile it.
8. **Release evidence:** if a later authorized implementation recompiles a release, renew its page-by-page visual QA and applicable hash-bound records. Preserve the reviewed inputs. This plan authorizes no release or modification itself.

These checks propose an implementable review gate; none is a claim that the redesign has been produced or passed.
