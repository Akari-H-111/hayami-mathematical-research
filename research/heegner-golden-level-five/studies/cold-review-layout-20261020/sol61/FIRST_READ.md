## Incoming reader's impression

This has the local typography of a formal mathematics paper, but the reading rhythm of an illustrated lecture or a mathematical atlas interleaved with a short paper. The distinction matters: the mathematical prose looks serious, while the page allocation repeatedly tells me that the illustrations are the main event. I would expect substantial restructuring before treating this as a finished research article.

The title and abstract on PDF page 1 identify an interesting proposed chain: a golden coordinate from a ruled surface, an arithmetic recovery at a CM value, a reflection related to finite Fourier symmetry, and an analytic functional equation. The abstract communicates the ingredients better than it identifies the principal new result. As a graduate reader I want an early statement explaining what is proved here, what is classical input, and why this particular chain is more than a collection of occurrences of the same scalar.

Page 1 is dense but recognizably mathematical. It introduces the surface, derives a rank-loss coordinate, and sends me to Figures 1–5. I then spend pages 2–6 in landscape illustrations before reaching the first numbered theorem on page 7. That is the first major break in attention. The connection map is useful, and a surface picture is welcome, but a pentagon and an all-chords icosahedron do not each need the same page status as the argument they support. By page 6 I have lost the thread of the surface calculation and am waiting for the paper to begin again.

The same pattern recurs. Theorem 2.1 and its short proof occupy the upper part of page 7, followed by two figure pages (8–9). The common involution and Proposition 3.1 occupy page 10, followed by three figure pages (11–13). Page 14 begins with the isolated continuation “Also” before reaching the Poisson–Mellin bridge. Its proof is followed by two more figure pages (15–16), so the classification theorem on page 17 arrives after another detour. The page-10 to page-14 break is especially damaging: the return feels like recovering a paragraph interrupted by a slide deck.

## What the pictures do for me

Figure 1 (page 2) is the best orientation device. Figure 2 (page 3) gives geometric substance to the surface formula. Figure 8 (page 11) makes coordinate exchange and the two reflection axes easier to understand. Figure 10 (page 13) helps fix the residue order and sign convention. These have identifiable reader jobs.

Other pages devote much of their space to a simple plot plus formulas or annotations already stated in the prose. Figure 4 (page 5), Figure 6 (page 8), and Figure 11 (page 15) are conspicuous examples. Their whitespace is orderly, but it does not create a corresponding increase in information. Figure 7 (page 9) emphasizes many decimal digits and a residual graph; without the computational context in view, that precision draws attention away from the theorem. Figure 5 (page 6) preserves useful chord information, but the many overlapping segments are harder to read than the counts beside them.

The consistent gold/teal/violet/blue vocabulary is attractive. Axes and displayed formulas generally appear clear at the supplied image size. The recurring tiny color-key footer and small legends contribute less than their repetition suggests. Serif article text and sans-serif figure panels are individually serviceable, but the large panels with their own headings, formula blocks, and repeated key look like presentation slides inserted into an article. The sideways page numbers on landscape sheets reinforce the change of reading mode.

## Editor's initial verdict

The paper has formal mathematical components: definitions, equations, named results, proofs, appendices, and references. It does not yet have convincing article proportions or a stable narrative hierarchy. Twelve illustration sheets dominate a nineteen-page object, while important theorem prose is sparse or interrupted. I would keep the explanatory ambition and change the distribution of attention.

The concluding material on pages 17–19 is more compact and informative. It distinguishes the five-periodic classification, the exact certificate, and the odd-N extension. The appendix contains meaningful technical detail rather than decorative bulk. Nevertheless, the main contribution becomes clearer retrospectively than at the entrance. The pages seem intended to serve both an illustrated explanatory atlas and a formal paper; each purpose would be served better if its role were explicit.
