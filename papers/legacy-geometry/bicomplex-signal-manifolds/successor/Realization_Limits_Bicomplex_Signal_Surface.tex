\begin{abstract}
Earlier versions of this work asserted that a moment map and a power
constraint, followed by a ``geometric projection'' $\R^4\to\R^3$, produce an
explicit ruled surface $S$. We prove that the historical moment assignment
$(|z_1|^2,|z_2|^2)=(\cos t,1-\cos t)$ admits no readout that is
real-analytic along the circle $\{z_1=0\}$, whatever family of states is
used. This excludes affine projections, real polynomial maps and every
polynomial in a bicomplex variable and its conjugates. Phase-invariant and
mixed-state readouts fail without any regularity hypothesis. The obstruction
is sharp in three ways. An explicit continuous state family with an explicit
$C^\infty$ readout realizes $S$. Each half of the source admits a
real-analytic readout but no affine one. A different power assignment admits
a phase-sensitive quadratic readout.

We then separate the carriers of the observation-field monodromy. The
square-root cover gives a rank-two local system that is indecomposable over
$\Z$ and splits only after inverting $2$. It differs from the Whitney fold
involution and from the normalization defect, and the constant sheaf carries
no $\pm1$ decomposition. For every continuous map from a convex parameter
domain to the plane, the exceptional pullback of $\Z$ is the extension by
zero from the interior. Folds are detected instead by failure of the
comparison map that defines cohomological smoothness. Finally, we record the
residual geometry and spectral facts that survive, with their exact scope.
An appendix corrects all 66 named statements of the earlier version, one
statement specific to its first public file, and the earlier public
description, item by item.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The preprint \emph{Geometric Realization of Bicomplex Signal Manifolds} has
two public files, v11 and v12 \cite{record}. It studied an explicit ruled map
$S$, together with a signal-theoretic narrative: a power-conserving
two-component state, a moment map, a projection to three-dimensional
observations, and a family of spectral, sheaf-theoretic and arithmetic
interpretations. A complete audit of the 66 named statements of v12, together
with new proofs, shows that the geometry of $S$ is rich and largely correct.
Several of the interpretive bridges, however, are either unsupported or
false. This paper replaces that record. Its own new results concern the step
the title of the earlier work promised and never supplied: \emph{which
readouts of which state families realize $S$?}

\paragraph{Main results.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Analytic rigidity} (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}). Under the
historical assignment $|z_1|^2=\cos t$, every state over the two edges
$t=\pm\pi/2$ lies on the circle $C_0=\{z_1=0\}$, and those edges map to a
non-collinear ``V''. Consequently no readout that is real-analytic along $C_0$
realizes $S$, for any state family, on the closed source or on the source
open in $t$.
\item \emph{Sharpness}. An explicit $C^\infty$ readout realizes $S$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}). On one half of the source, real-analytic readouts
exist but affine ones do not (Proposition~\ref{prop:half}). A different power
assignment admits a phase-sensitive quadratic readout
(Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}). Phase-invariant readouts and readouts of
mixed states fail outright (Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge}). Remark~\ref{rem:data}
gives an edge criterion that explains all of these.
\item \emph{Carriers of the monodromy} (Proposition~\ref{prop:carrier}). The
two-traversal restoration of $\sqrt F$ lives on a local system that is
indecomposable over $\Z$. It is distinct from the fold involution and from
the normalization defect.
\item \emph{Exceptional pullbacks} (Theorem~\ref{thm:shriek} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:detect}). $\Psi^!\Z$ depends only on the convex
parameter domain, not on the map; the earlier fold formula
$i^*\Psi^!\Z\simeq\Z[-1]$ holds at no point.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:residual}--\ref{sec:limits} retain the correct residual
geometry, spectral estimates and scope limits of v12, in sharpened form.
Appendix~\ref{app:corrections} lists every correction.

\paragraph{What ``bicomplex'' means here.} The bicomplex algebra is isomorphic,
through its idempotents, to $\C\oplus\C$. Its three standard conjugations
become componentwise conjugation and the exchange of the two components. No
proof below uses bicomplex multiplication beyond this identification. That is
itself one of the outcomes: readouts built from bicomplex multiplication and
conjugations are real polynomial maps, so Corollary~\ref{cor:excluded}
excludes them as well. We keep the name for continuity with the earlier
record.

\paragraph{Relation to other work.} The completed source atlas, the five rank
singularities, the dipole indices and the fold classification of the planar
observation map are proved for the same surface in the published papers
\cite{ruled,stokes}. We cite them rather than re-prove them. The scalar
Green, Markov and Krein theory of the Laplace--Beltrami operator, and the
corrected two-point extension problem that replaces v12 Propositions
8.13--8.16, belong to a separate companion paper \cite{companion}. Standard
frameworks are credited where used. The new contributions are the model
results named above. Their priority relative to the literature has not been
systematically established.

\paragraph{Evidence.} Every statement carries one of the following kinds of
evidence: a written proof; a cited external theorem with its hypotheses
checked; an exact computer-algebra identity; a finite numerical replay; or a
Lean-checked statement. Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} states which applies to
which result. No result rests on a historical ``proven'' label.

\section{The surface, its source and the observation field}\label{sec:model}
Throughout,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
 S(t,u)=\bigl(c+2u(1-c),\,(1-u)s,\,uQ\bigr),\qquad
 c=\cos t,\ s=\sin t,\ Q=\sqrt{c(2-c)},
\end{equation}
on the source $D=[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\times[0,1]$. Put
$D_\circ=(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\times[0,1]$ and $D_+=[0,\pi/2]\times[0,1]$. The edges
and the polar ruling map to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edges}
 S(\pm\tfrac\pi2,u)=(2u,\pm(1-u),0),\qquad S(0,u)=(1,0,u).
\end{equation}
The two edge segments lie on distinct lines through the apex $(2,0,0)$. The
ruling line $\{(1,0,z)\}$ is skew to both edge lines.

\begin{proposition}[Completed source and five rank labels \cite{ruled}]\label{prop:atlas}
Near $t=\epsilon\pi/2$, $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, let $r=Q\geq0$ and
$C(r)=1-\sqrt{1-r^2}$, and put
$B_\epsilon(r,u)=(C+2u(1-C),\epsilon(1-u)\sqrt{1-C^2},ur)$. This extends
smoothly to $|r|<1$ and agrees with $S$ on the physical overlap. The rank-loss
labels of the completed source are exactly
\[
 P_0=(0,1),\quad P_\pm=(\pm t_0,u_0),\quad E_\pm=(\pm\pi/2,0),\qquad
 c_0=\tfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\ t_0=\arccos c_0,\ u_0=\tfrac{1-c_0}{2}.
\]
Their smooth extensions are cross-caps. The adapted determinants are
$\Delta(P_0)=1$, $\Delta(P_\pm)=-5\sqrt{\sqrt5-2}$ and $-\epsilon$ at $E_\pm$.
The physical restrictions at $P_0$ and $E_\pm$ are a boundary half-germ and
corner quarter-germs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$C(r)(2-C(r))=r^2$ gives $C(Q)=c$, and differentiation gives
$(B_\epsilon)_r\times(B_\epsilon)_u=(\epsilon u,2u,0)$ at $r=0$. On $c>0$ the
cross-product equations reduce to $c=1,u=1$, or to $u=(1-c)/2=c(2-c)/2$, that
is $c^2-3c+1=0$. Since $S_{uu}=0$, the cross-cap determinant reduces to
$\det(S_u,S_{ut},S_{tt})$, and the smooth recognition criterion
\cite[Cor.~4.5]{hasegawa} applies. Full proofs and Lean-checked rank statements
are in \cite{ruled}.
\end{proof}
Thus $c^2-3c+1$ selects only the two lateral labels. On
$D_{\rm reg}=D\setminus\{P_0,P_\pm,E_\pm\}$, a connected orientable surface
with corners, $H_1(D_{\rm reg};\Z)=\Z^2$.

\paragraph{The observation field.} On the angular chart $|t|<\pi/2$ the normal
$V=S_t\times S_u$ has components $V_z=N$ and $V_x=M$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 N=(1-c)(1-c-2u),\qquad
 M=\frac{c^2(2-c)-u(1-c+c^2)}{Q},\qquad F=N+iM,\qquad \Psi=(N,M).
\end{equation}
Both components are even in $t$. Because $Q=0$ at $t=\pm\pi/2$, $M$ is
unbounded there for $u>0$. The field $F$ and the map $\Psi$ are therefore
defined on $D_\circ$, not on $D$. They also depend on the chart: a
reparametrization of the source multiplies $V$ by its Jacobian.

\section{Realizations of the surface by signal states}\label{sec:realization}
Let $S^3=\{z\in\C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1\}$, and let $C_0=\{(0,w):|w|=1\}$ and
$C_1=\{(w,0):|w|=1\}$. The moment map $z\mapsto(|z_1|^2,|z_2|^2)$ sends $S^3$
onto the segment $X+Y=1$. By itself it defines neither a two-dimensional
source, nor a lift of \eqref{eq:S}, nor a projection taking that lift to $S$.
A realization therefore consists of two pieces of data.

\begin{definition}\label{def:realization}
Let $D'\subset D$. A \emph{state family} on $D'$ is any map $z:D'\to S^3$; no
continuity is assumed. A \emph{readout} is a map $P:U\to\R^3$ defined on a set
$U\subset\C^2$ containing $z(D')$. The pair \emph{realizes} $S$ if
$P\circ z=S$ on $D'$. The family is \emph{moment-compatible} (with the
historical assignment) if $|z_1(t,u)|^2=\cos t$. A readout is
\emph{real-analytic along $C_0$} if $C_0\subset U$ and
$\beta\mapsto P(0,e^{i\beta})$ is real-analytic.
\end{definition}

\subsection{A realization with varying powers}
The following realization exists, but with powers that differ from the
historical ones.
\begin{theorem}[Quadrature realization]\label{thm:realization}
Put $\zeta_+(t)=e^{it/2}$ and $\zeta_-(t)=\sqrt{1-c/2}+i\sqrt{c/2}$. The state
family and the fixed real quadratic readout
\[
 \Phi(t,u)=\bigl(\sqrt{1-u}\,\zeta_+(t),\ \sqrt u\,\zeta_-(t)\bigr),\qquad
 P(z)=\bigl(\Re z_1^2+|z_2|^2+\Re z_2^2,\ \Im z_1^2,\ \Im z_2^2\bigr)
\]
satisfy $|\Phi_1|^2=1-u$, $|\Phi_2|^2=u$ and $P\circ\Phi=S$ on all of $D$. The
family is smooth for $0<u<1$ in the completed charts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\zeta_+^2=c+is$ and $\zeta_-^2=1-c+iQ$, so
$P\Phi=((1-u)c+u(2-c),(1-u)s,uQ)=S$. In the edge chart the imaginary part of
$\zeta_-$ is $r/\sqrt{2(2-C)}$ and its real part is $\sqrt{1-C/2}$; both are
smooth.
\end{proof}
The required phase coupling is model data; power conservation does not
select it.

\begin{proposition}[Two obstructions]\label{prop:twoobs}
(1) No $C^1$ state family can have $|z_2|^2=u$ up to $u=0$, or $|z_1|^2=1-u$ up
to $u=1$.
(2) No three fixed Hermitian $2\times2$ matrices $H_j$ and normalized pure-state
family satisfy $S_j=z^*H_jz$ on $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) At a zero of a $C^1$ component $w$, the derivative of $|w|^2$ is
$2\Re(\overline w\,\partial w)=0$, while $\partial_u u=1$.
(2) $z^*H_jz=a_j+(Mr(z))_j$, where $r(z)$ is the Bloch vector, a point of the
unit sphere, and $M$ is a real $3\times3$ matrix. If $\rank M\leq2$ the image
is planar, but the values $(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,-1,0),(1,0,1)$ of $S$ have
affine determinant $2$. If $M$ is invertible the image is an ellipsoid, which
contains no segment, but $S(0,u)=(1,0,u)$ is one. See also \cite{bloch}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The historical assignment}
Every state with $t=\pm\pi/2$ lies on $C_0$, and every state with $t=0$ lies
on $C_1$.
\begin{proposition}[Phase-invariant and mixed-state readouts]\label{prop:gauge}
Suppose $D'$ contains two distinct points of one edge $\{\pm\pi/2\}\times[0,1]$.
Then no readout with $P(e^{i\gamma}z)=P(z)$ realizes $S$ on $D'$ with a
moment-compatible family. In particular no function of $zz^*$ does,
including all Hermitian expectation values. If trace-one density matrices
$\rho\geq0$ with $\rho_{11}=\cos t$ replace pure states, no function of $\rho$
realizes $S$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$C_0$ is a single orbit of the global phase, so such a readout is constant on
$C_0$, whereas $S$ takes distinct values at the two edge points. For density
matrices, $\rho_{11}=0$ and positivity force $\rho_{12}=0$, so
$\rho=\mathrm{diag}(0,1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Analytic curves and lines]\label{lem:analytic}
Let $p:J\to\R^3$ be real-analytic on an open interval or on the circle. For
every line $\ell$, either $p(J)\subset\ell$ or $p(J)\cap\ell$ is countable. In
particular $p(J)$ cannot contain uncountable subsets of two distinct lines.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $x_0\in\ell$ and unit normals $n_1,n_2$ to $\ell$. If
$p(J)\not\subset\ell$, some $n_k\cdot(p-x_0)$ is a nonzero real-analytic
function. Its zero set is discrete, hence countable, and contains
$p^{-1}(\ell)$. Two distinct lines meet in at most one point.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Analytic rigidity]\label{thm:rigidity}
Let a moment-compatible family $z$ and a readout $P$ realize $S$ on $D'$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $D'=D$, then $P$ is not real-analytic along $C_0$. More generally, $P$
is not real-analytic on any connected open arc of $C_0$ that contains all edge
states $z(\pm\pi/2,u)$.
\item If $D'=D_\circ$, $C_0\subset U$ and $P|_{U\cap S^3}$ is continuous at each
point of $C_0$, then $P$ is not real-analytic along $C_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) The image of $C_0$, or of the arc, contains both edge segments of
\eqref{eq:edges}, which are uncountable subsets of distinct lines; this
contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic}.
(2) Fix $u$ and $\epsilon=\pm1$, and take $t_n\to\epsilon\pi/2$. A subsequence of
$z(t_n,u)\in S^3$ converges to some $z^*$ with $z^*_1=0$, that is $z^*\in C_0$.
Continuity gives $P(z^*)=\lim S(t_n,u)=S(\epsilon\pi/2,u)$, so both edge
segments lie in $P(C_0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Excluded projections]\label{cor:excluded}
Under the historical assignment on $D$ or $D_\circ$, $S$ is not realized by
any affine map $\R^4\to\R^3$, any real polynomial map, any polynomial in a
bicomplex variable and its conjugates, or any map real-analytic on a
neighbourhood of $C_0$. On $D$ this also excludes stereographic projection
from a point of $C_0$ that avoids the edge states. In particular, the
``Geometric Projection'' of v12 Definition~3.1 cannot be completed by any
such map.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Sharpness}
\begin{theorem}[$C^\infty$ realization]\label{thm:smooth}
Put $\theta=\pi/8$. Take smooth functions $\Lambda,\psi,\varrho,\kappa:[0,1]\to[0,1]$
such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Lambda=0$ on $[0,\frac14]$, $\Lambda=1$ on $[\frac34,1]$, and $\Lambda$ is
strictly increasing in between;
\item $\psi=0$ on $[0,\frac13]$ and $\psi=1$ on $[\frac23,1]$;
\item $\varrho=0$ on $[0,\frac34]$ and $\varrho=1$ on $[\frac78,1]$;
\item $\kappa=1$ on $[0,\frac18]$ and $\kappa=0$ on $[\frac14,1]$.
\end{itemize}
Let $\chi$ be a smooth $2\pi$-periodic function equal to $1$ on
$[-\frac\pi8,\frac{3\pi}8]$ and to $-1$ on $[\frac{7\pi}8,\frac{11\pi}8]$, and
let $\nu=0$ for $t\geq0$ and $\nu=1$ for $t<0$. Define
\[
 z(t,u)=\bigl(\sqrt c\,e^{i\theta\Lambda(c)u},\
 \sqrt{1-c}\,e^{i(\nu\pi+\theta(1-\Lambda(c))(1+u))}\bigr).
\]
This is a moment-compatible family. It is continuous on $D$, smooth on
$D_\circ$, and smooth up to $t=\pm\pi/2$ in the edge charts, since
$\sqrt{C(r)}=r/\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-r^2}}$. With $c=|z_1|^2$ and principal arguments,
set
\begin{gather*}
 \mathcal U=\psi(c)\frac{\arg z_1}{\theta\Lambda(c)}
 +(1-\psi(c))\Bigl(\frac{\arg z_2^2}{2\theta(1-\Lambda(c))}-1\Bigr),\\
 \Sigma=\varrho(c)\,\Re z_2\sqrt{1+c}+(1-\varrho(c))\,|z_2|\chi(\arg z_2)\sqrt{1+c},\\
 \mathcal Q=\kappa(c)\,\Re z_1\sqrt{2-c}+(1-\kappa(c))\sqrt{c(2-c)},
\end{gather*}
where each summand is read as zero where its cutoff vanishes. Then
$P=(|z_1|^2+2\,\mathcal U|z_2|^2,\ (1-\mathcal U)\Sigma,\ \mathcal U\mathcal Q)$
is smooth on an open neighbourhood of $z(D)$, and $P\circ z=S$ on $D$.
Multiplying by a smooth bump equal to $1$ near $z(D)$ gives a $C^\infty$
readout on $\C^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $c\geq\frac34$, $\Lambda=1$ and $z_2=\sgn(t)\sqrt{1-\cos t}=\sqrt2\sin(t/2)$,
which is smooth; at $t=\pm\pi/2$ only $\sqrt c\to0$ enters. On the image,
$\arg z_1=\theta\Lambda u\in[0,\theta]$,
$\arg z_2^2=2\theta(1-\Lambda)(1+u)\in[0,\pi/2]$, and
$\arg z_2\in\nu\pi+[0,\pi/4]$. Hence both decoders equal $u$ wherever they are
used. The first is used only for $c\geq\frac13$, where $\Lambda>0$ and
$z_1\neq0$. The second is used only for $c\leq\frac23$, where $\Lambda<1$ and
$|z_2|^2\geq\frac13$.

Where $\varrho>0$, $z_2$ is real and $\Re z_2\sqrt{1+c}=\sgn(t)\sqrt{1-c^2}=s$.
Where $\varrho<1$, $\chi(\arg z_2)=\sgn t$. Where $\kappa>0$, $z_1=\sqrt c$ and
$\Re z_1\sqrt{2-c}=Q$. Each summand is smooth on the union of two open sets:
one where its decoder is smooth, and one where its cutoff vanishes. The two
definitions agree on the overlap, and the union contains the image.
Substitution gives $P\circ z=S$; compactness of the image provides the bump.
\end{proof}
A finite replay on $801\times81$ labels reproduces $S$ to $1.4\times10^{-14}$.
On $C_0$ the readout traces the two legs of the V on disjoint arcs, joined
through the flat function $\chi$. That is exactly what
Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic} forbids for analytic readouts.

\begin{proposition}[One half of the source]\label{prop:half}
(1) If $D'$ contains at least three points of the ruling $\{0\}\times[0,1]$
and at least three points of one edge, then no affine readout realizes $S$ on
$D'$ with a moment-compatible family. This applies in particular to $D_+$,
which contains the single interior cross-cap $P_+$.
(2) On $D_+$ there exist a real-analytic moment-compatible family and a readout
that is real-analytic on a neighbourhood of its image and realizes $S$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) Write $P=b+L$. On $C_1$ the readout traces an ellipse centred at $b$,
possibly degenerate, through three collinear points of the ruling line. A
nondegenerate ellipse meets a line in at most two points, so the ellipse is a
segment, and $b$ lies on the ruling line. Applying the same argument on $C_0$
puts $b$ on the edge line. The two lines are skew.

(2) Fix $0<\theta<\pi$ and put $z=e^{i\theta u}v(t)$, with
$v=(\sqrt{\cos t},\sqrt2\sin(t/2))$. In the edge chart,
$v=(r/\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-r^2}},(1-r^2)^{1/4})$. Both expressions extend
analytically across $t=0$ and $r=0$, and so does $S$ in the completed charts.
Since $\partial_uz=i\theta z$ and $\partial_tz=e^{i\theta u}v'$ with
$v'\neq0$ real, $z$ is an immersion. If $e^{i\theta u}v=e^{i\theta u'}v'$
with $v,v'$ nonnegative, the phase quotient is positive, so $u=u'$ and
$t=t'$. An immersion that is injective on a compact set embeds a
neighbourhood of it. The nearest-point retraction onto a real-analytic embedded
surface is real-analytic on a tube, by the analytic inverse function theorem
for $(x,n)\mapsto x+n$ on the normal bundle. Composing the retraction, the
inverse embedding and the extended $S$ gives the readout.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Edge criterion and the extra data a realization needs]\label{rem:data}
Lemma~\ref{lem:analytic} yields a necessary criterion for any power assignment
$w=|z_1|^2$. An analytic readout requires $S(w^{-1}(0))$ to lie in the image
of an analytic loop, and a phase-invariant readout requires it to be a
single point; the same holds for $1-w$. The assignment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:realization} passes. Its vanishing edges $u=1$ and $u=0$ map
to arcs of the circles $(x-1)^2+z^2=1$ and $x^2+y^2=1$, and its readout maps
the two vanishing circles onto exactly these circles. The historical
assignment fails because $w^{-1}(0)$ consists of both edges.

A realization of the historical model therefore needs data the moment map does
not supply. One option is a non-analytic switching readout, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}. Another is to keep only half of the source, which
loses a cross-cap and the reflection pairing $S(t,1)=S(-t,1)$. A third is to
change the powers. Approximation carries no content: by the Weierstrass
theorem, polynomial readouts satisfy $\sup_D|P_\varepsilon\circ z-S|<\varepsilon$
for every $\varepsilon>0$, yet none is exact. Whether a quadratic readout
exists on $D_+$ is open.
\end{remark}

\section{Observation monodromy and its carriers}\label{sec:monodromy}
\subsection{Interior dipole and square-root restoration}
\begin{theorem}[Dipole and two-traversal restoration]\label{thm:dipole}
The interior zeros of $F$ in $D_\circ$ are exactly $P_\pm$, and $F(0,1)=0$ at
the boundary. With the $(t,u)$ orientation,
$\det DF(P_\pm)=\mp5s_0(1-c_0)^2/Q_0$ and $\operatorname{Ind}(P_\pm)=\mp1$
\cite{ruled}. Every continuous branch of $\sqrt F$ transported round a small
positive loop about either point changes sign, and two traversals restore it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$N=0$ exactly on $c=1$ and on $u=(1-c)/2$. On the first, $M=1-u$; on the
second, $M$ is a positive multiple of $-(c^2-3c+1)$. The determinant follows
from $c_0^2=3c_0-1$, and a nondegenerate zero has small-circle winding equal
to the sign of the determinant. Write $F(\gamma(s))=\exp L(s)$ with a
continuous logarithm, so that $L(1)=L(0)+2\pi in$ with $n=\pm1$. Then
$\chi=\exp(L/2)$ satisfies $\chi(1)=-\chi(0)$. Any other continuous root
differs from $\chi$ by a constant sign.
\end{proof}
This is a count of traversals. It assigns no physical clock, and it does not
identify the root cover with any spin structure. Earlier versions mixed this
with two other involutions, which Proposition~\ref{prop:carrier} separates.
The value $|\det DF(P_\pm)|\approx2.245$ is not $\sqrt5$. The number $\sqrt5$
is $|P'(c_0)|$ for the monic $P(c)=c^2-3c+1$, and it changes when $P$ is
rescaled. Both $N$ and $M$ are even in $t$, so the sign reversal of the
Jacobian comes from the $t$-derivatives, not from parity of the components.

\subsection{The polar fold}
\begin{proposition}[Polar fold]\label{prop:polar}
The smooth extension of $\Psi$ at $P_0=(0,1)$ is a Whitney fold of local
degree zero. No interior winding index is assigned on the physical
half-neighbourhood.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the source coordinates $a=-M$ and $b=2\sin(t/2)\sqrt{u-(1-\cos t)/2}$,
and the target coordinates $(N,M)\mapsto(-M,-N)$. The derivative matrix at
$(0,1)$ is invertible, and the exact identity $-N=b^2$ gives the normal form
$(a,b^2)$. Reflecting $b$ pairs preimages of opposite orientation.
\end{proof}
The remainder displayed in v12 omitted $M(t,1)=-t^4/2+O(t^6)$, but the exact
charts above do not depend on it. The full interior critical locus of $\Psi$
consists of the symmetry segment $t=0$, which is an ordinary fold for $u>0$,
and a rational branch, which consists of ordinary folds away from $(0,0)$; at
$(0,0)$ the ordinary-fold condition fails \cite[Thm.~2.4]{stokes}.

\subsection{Three different sign structures}
\begin{proposition}[Carriers]\label{prop:carrier}
(1) For connected $X$, $\End_{D(X)}(\Z_X)=H^0(X;\Z)=\Z$. Every involution of
$\Z_X$ is therefore $\pm\mathrm{id}$, $\Z_X$ admits no splitting into two
nonzero summands, and its characteristic polynomial is $t-1$ or $t+1$. The
same holds for the constant condensed sheaf on a compact connected space,
whose global sections are the continuous maps to $\Z$.

(2) Let $X=D_\circ\setminus\{P_+,P_-,(0,1)\}$ and let $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$ be
the double cover $\{\chi^2=F\}$. Then $\pi_*\Z$ is a rank-two local system
with deck involution $\tau$, indecomposable over $\Z$. After inverting $2$,
$\pi_*\Z[\frac12]\simeq\Z[\frac12]\oplus\mathcal L[\frac12]$, where the sign
system $\mathcal L$ has monodromy $-1$ around each of $P_\pm$.

(3) A Whitney fold involution $(\xi,\eta)\mapsto(\xi,-\eta)$ fixes the fold arc
pointwise, whereas $\tau$ is fixed-point free; neither induces the other.

(4) For a standard cross-cap image $X'$ with topological normalization
$\nu$, the rational normalization defect
$\mathcal Q_\nu=\nu_*\Q_{\widetilde X'}/\Q_{X'}\simeq j_!\mathcal L_{\rm sgn}$
lives on the image double ray and has zero stalk at the pinch. Every ordinary
morphism from $\mathcal Q_\nu$ to a skyscraper at the pinch is zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) Degree-zero endomorphisms of a sheaf in degree zero are the global
sections of the sheaf $\mathcal{H}om(\Z,\Z)=\Z$. The only idempotents of $\Z$ are $0$ and $1$.
(2) $D_\circ\setminus\{(0,1)\}$ is star-shaped about an interior point, so
$\pi_1(X)$ is free on loops around $P_\pm$, on which $F$ winds $\mp1$. The
monodromy of $\pi$ is therefore the surjection onto $C_2$, and the stalk is
the regular module $\Z[C_2]=\Z[x]/(x^2-1)$, whose only idempotents are $0$
and $1$. Over $\Z[\frac12]$ the projectors $(1\pm x)/2$ split it.
(3) The fixed-point sets differ.
(4) This is the stalk computation of v12 Proposition~8.6 \cite{stacks}: the
diagonal stalk map is an isomorphism at one-preimage points and has an
anti-invariant one-dimensional quotient at two-preimage points.
\end{proof}
Hence the ``rank-two $\pm1$ decomposition of the constant sheaf'' stated in
the earlier public description has no carrier. The correct $-1$ sector is
$\mathcal L$, which lives on the punctured source and records the winding of
$F$. The normalization sign line lives instead on the image double stratum
and records the exchange of sheets. The present data supply no canonical
isomorphism between these two $\Z/2$-torsors. No map from a Mellin Hilbert
space to either carrier has been constructed.

\subsection{Exceptional pullbacks do not see folds}
We use the six-functor formalism on finite-dimensional locally compact
Hausdorff spaces with $D(X)=D(\mathrm{Sh}(X;\Z))$ \cite[Ex.~5.9]{scholze},
$\omega_X=a_X^!\Z$ and $\mathbb D_X=\RHom(-,\omega_X)$. Three standard facts
are needed:
\begin{itemize}
\item Verdier duality, $f^!\mathbb D_Y\simeq\mathbb D_Xf^*$
\cite[Prop.~4.6.9]{knp}, \cite[Ch.~III]{ks};
\item the stalk of $\omega_X$ at $x$ is the local homology
$H_*(X,X\setminus x)$, and $\omega_X\simeq\orr_X[n]$ on an $n$-manifold
\cite[Rem.~4.6.19, Cor.~4.6.20]{knp};
\item open immersions satisfy $j^!=j^*$ and are cohomologically smooth
\cite[Rem.~5.4]{scholze}.
\end{itemize}
A map $f$ is cohomologically smooth when the comparison
$f^!\Z\otimes f^*(-)\to f^!(-)$ is an isomorphism, $f^!\Z$ is invertible, and
both properties survive base change \cite[Def.~5.1]{scholze}.

\begin{theorem}[$f^!\Z$ sees only dimension and boundary]\label{thm:shriek}
(1) For any continuous $f:X\to Y$ between topological manifolds without
boundary of dimensions $n$ and $m$, $f^!\Z_Y\simeq\orr_X\otimes f^*\orr_Y[n-m]$.

(2) Let $D'\subset\R^2$ be locally closed and convex with nonempty interior,
and let $j:\inte D'\to D'$ be the inclusion. For every continuous
$\Psi:D'\to\R^2$, $\Psi^!\Z\simeq j_!\Z_{\inte D'}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) Since $\omega_Y$ is invertible, $\Z_Y\simeq\mathbb D_Y\omega_Y$, so
$f^!\Z_Y\simeq\mathbb D_Xf^*\omega_Y=\RHom(f^*\orr_Y[m],\orr_X[n])$.
(2) At a non-interior point $x$, $D'\setminus x$ is star-shaped about an
interior point, so the local homology vanishes. Hence
$\omega_{D'}\simeq j_!\Z_{\inte D'}[2]$, and
$\Psi^!\Z\simeq\mathbb D_{D'}(\Z_{D'}[2])=\omega_{D'}[-2]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:shift}
For $\Psi$ of \eqref{eq:F} on $D_\circ$ and any inclusion $i:L\to D_\circ$, the
complex $i^*\Psi^!\Z$ is $\Z$ in degree $0$ on $L\cap\inte D_\circ$ and $0$ on
the sides $u=0,1$. In particular $i^*\Psi^!\Z\simeq\Z[-1]$ holds at no point,
fold or otherwise. For an embedded open arc $L$ in the interior,
$i^!\Psi^!\Z\simeq\orr_L[-1]$; this shift is the codimension of $L$, not a
feature of folds.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Where the fold is visible]\label{prop:detect}
Let $x$ be an interior ordinary fold point of $\Psi$, and choose disc
neighbourhoods $V\ni x$ and $W\ni\Psi(x)$ on which $f=\Psi|_V$ is homeomorphic
to $(\xi,\eta)\mapsto(\xi,\eta^2)$. Let $L$ be the fold arc, let $W'\subset W$
be the open side of the discriminant containing $f(V\setminus L)$, and put
$G=j'_!\Z_{W'}$. Then $f^!\Z_W\simeq\Z_V$, $f^*G\simeq(\Z_{V\setminus L})_!$
and $f^!G\simeq\Z_V$. The comparison map $f^!\Z_W\otimes f^*G\to f^!G$ is
injective with cokernel $i_*\Z_L$. Thus the comparison condition of
cohomological smoothness fails at every ordinary fold point, while
invertibility of $f^!\Z$ holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the closed side $k:H\to W$, local homology vanishes on its edge, so
$\mathbb D_W(\Z_H)=k_*\omega_H\simeq G[2]$. Since $f(V)\subset H$,
$f^!G[2]\simeq\mathbb D_V(f^*\Z_H)=\Z_V[2]$. Off $L$, $f$ is locally an open
immersion, so the comparison map is an isomorphism there. A morphism
$(\Z_{V\setminus L})_!\to\Z_V$ is a locally constant function on $V\setminus L$,
here with values $\pm1$. Its cokernel is $i_*\Z_L$.
\end{proof}
The fold is also visible in $f_*\Z_V$, whose stalk ranks are $2,1,0$ across
the discriminant, and in the normalization defect on the surface side. A
compact Hausdorff image defines a condensed set. That fact alone, however,
supplies neither a canonical structure sheaf nor a singularity detector
(v12 Remark~8.7).

\section{Residual geometry}\label{sec:residual}
\subsection{Sublevel law and resolution cost}
For the standard models take Lebesgue measure and the residuals
$K_f=x^2+y^4$ and $K_\times=x^2(1+y^2)+y^4$.
\begin{theorem}[Sublevel law]\label{thm:sublevel}
With $B=B(\frac14,\frac32)$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
$V_f(\varepsilon)=B\varepsilon^{3/4}$ and
$B\varepsilon^{3/4}(1+\sqrt\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\leq V_\times(\varepsilon)\leq B\varepsilon^{3/4}$.
The exponent $3/4$ survives smooth changes of density and of coordinates; the
coefficient does not.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Slicing in $y$ gives $4\int_0^{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\sqrt{\varepsilon-y^4}\dd y$.
Substituting $y=\varepsilon^{1/4}v$ and then $z=v^4$ yields $B\varepsilon^{3/4}$.
For the cross-cap, the extra factor $(1+y^2)^{-1/2}$ lies between
$(1+\sqrt\varepsilon)^{-1/2}$ and $1$.
\end{proof}
The geometric cost $-\log V(\varepsilon)=\frac34\log\varepsilon^{-1}-\log B+o(1)$
is a resolution cost, not a channel capacity. Identical binary labels admit a
constant stochastic channel of capacity $0$ and a noiseless one of capacity
$\log2$. The residual supplies neither the kernel nor the resource
constraint. The earlier conjecture $C=\sqrt5\ln S$ is withdrawn.

\subsection{An explicitly chosen complex completion}
\begin{theorem}[Chosen $A_3$ completion]\label{thm:a3}
After its coefficients are extended to $\C$, the germ of $K_\times$ is
right-equivalent to $X^2+Y^4$, with Milnor and Tjurina numbers $3$. The fiber
$U=\{X^2+Y^4=1\}$ is a smooth genus-one curve $E$ with two points removed.
$H^1(U)$ has dimension three and monodromy spectrum $\{-i,1,i\}$. The weight-graded
pieces of its mixed Hodge structure have dimension two in weight one and one
in weight two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The substitution $X=x\sqrt{1+y^2}$, $Y=y$ is a holomorphic coordinate change
with invertible derivative. The weighted compactification
$X^2+Y^4=Z^4\subset\mathbb P(2,1,1)$ is a double cover of $\mathbb P^1$
branched at four points, hence of genus one. Its two ends at infinity are
unramified. Weighted homogeneity identifies the global and local Milnor
fibers \cite{milnor,dimca}. The monodromy $h(X,Y)=(-X,iY)$ acts by $-i$ and
$i$ on $H^1(E)$ and fixes the boundary class. The Gysin sequence
$0\to H^1(E)\to H^1(U)\to\widetilde H^0(\{\infty_\pm\})(-1)\to0$ gives the
weights \cite{deligne,peters}.
\end{proof}
These weights belong to the chosen complexification. They are not inferred
from the real surface or from a Mellin parameter. The monodromy alone does not
determine the specialization and variation maps of a nearby-cycle package.

\subsection{Three different projected curves}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curves}
(1) For each $r>0$, the real link $\{K_\times=r^2\}$ is a circle. Its
cross-cap image has one transverse double point, so it is a figure-eight
graph. The lift $(x,xy,y^2,y)$ is an embedding.
(2) The weights of $X^2+Y^4$ are $(2,1)$. For $a,b>0$ with $a^2+b^4=1$, the
orbit $\Gamma(s)=(ae^{2is},be^{is})$ is embedded, and its shadow
$(b\sin s,a\sin2s)$ is a transverse figure-eight with frequency ratio $2:1$.
The action $2\pi(4a^2+b^2)$ has infimum $2\pi$ over nondegenerate orbits; it
is attained only in their closure, and $b^2=\frac18$ is a strict maximum.
(3) For the actual lateral germs the Euclidean Whitney invariants satisfy
$b_\pm^2+a_\pm c_\pm^2=0$. The exceptional principal-curvature trace has an
ordinary cusp at the double return, not a node \cite[Prop.~2.1]{principal}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
(1) The gradient of $K_\times$ vanishes only at $0$. Equal images force
$x=x'$ and $y'=\pm y$, and the tangent images at $(0,\pm\sqrt r)$ are
transverse.
(2) The $Y$ coordinate fixes the phase. With $z=b^2$ the action divided by
$2\pi$ is $1+(1-z)(4z+3)$.
(3) The invariants are computed exactly from the actual second jet, and the
pedal trace satisfies $\det(p'',p''')\neq0$ at its double return. The
computation is in the exact replay of Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}.
\end{proof}
These three constructions yield distinct curves. No amplitude, clock or
scattering rule transfers from one to another.

\section{Spectral and arithmetic statements that survive}\label{sec:spectral}
These results are standard. We record them because the earlier versions
stated them with gaps in measure, domain or hypotheses.

\paragraph{Mellin operators.} Let $T_\sigma f=-i(xf'+\sigma f)$. For $C^1$
functions on $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$,
$\int_a^b(\overline{T_\sigma f}g-\overline fT_{1-\sigma}g)\dd x=i[x\overline fg]_a^b$.
In $L^2(dx)$ the formal adjoint is therefore $T_{1-\sigma}$, which equals
$T_\sigma$ exactly at $\sigma=\frac12$. For the measure $x^{2\beta-1}\dd x$ the
formal adjoint parameter is $2\beta-\sigma$ instead. Formal symmetry is not
self-adjointness. The map $(Uf)(s)=e^{s/2}f(e^s)$ is unitary from
$L^2([1,\infty),dx)$ onto $L^2([0,\infty),ds)$, and it carries the closure of
$T_{1/2}$ on $C_c^\infty$ to $-i\partial_s$ on $H^1_0$, whose adjoint has
domain $H^1$. The deficiency indices are $(1,0)$, so no self-adjoint extension
exists. On the full multiplicative line the operator is self-adjoint
\cite{reed}. The functions $x^{-\sigma-i\omega}$ satisfy
$T_\sigma x^{-\sigma-i\omega}=-\omega x^{-\sigma-i\omega}$. They lie in
$L^2([1,\infty))$ exactly when $\sigma>\frac12$, and even then they violate
the minimal zero trace. Real formal eigenvalues imply no orthogonality.

\paragraph{Finite Gram matrices.} For distinct real $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_M$,
$T>0$ and $G_{jk}=T^{-1}\int_0^Te^{i(\omega_j-\omega_k)t}\dd t$, the phasors are
linearly independent and $G$ is positive definite for \emph{every} $T>0$; a
Vandermonde argument at an interior time proves this. With
$\varepsilon=2(M-1)/(T\min|\omega_j-\omega_k|)<1$, the spectrum of $G$ lies in
$[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]$, $\kappa_2(G)\leq(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)$
and $M\log(1-\varepsilon)\leq\log\det G\leq0$. The earlier threshold
$T>2(M-1)/\delta$ is thus sufficient for conditioning but not needed for
independence. These estimates concern a fixed finite family.

\paragraph{Hardy evaluation and Bohr--Haar.} On the coefficient Hilbert space
of Dirichlet series, evaluation at $\Re s=\sigma$ is bounded exactly when
$\sigma>\frac12$, with norm $\zeta(2\sigma)^{1/2}$ \cite{hardy}. The Gram limit
$\lim T^{-1}\int_0^Tn^{it}m^{-it}\dd t=\delta_{nm}$ is character orthogonality
on the product torus of prime exponents. A finite set of integers may generate
a lattice of lower rank, as $\{2,4\}$ does, so no free group of rank $M$ is
involved. The half-density threshold and the evaluation threshold are two
separate results.

\begin{proposition}[Log-scale and linear-scale windows]\label{prop:windows}
Let $K(\delta,L)=L^{-1}\int_0^Le^{-i\delta\tau}\dd\tau$, so that
$|K|=|\sin(\delta L/2)|/(|\delta|L/2)\leq2/(|\delta|L)$, and let $T>1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\frac1{\log T}\int_1^Tx^{-i\delta}\frac{dx}x=K(\delta,\log T)$. The
log-scale and linear-scale bounds are one bound, $2/(|\delta|\cdot\text{mass})$,
evaluated at window masses $\log T$ and $T$; their ratio $T/\log T$ is the same
for every real $\delta$.
\item For the same characters on the same interval with Lebesgue measure,
$\frac1{T-1}\int_1^Tx^{-i\delta}\dd x=\frac{T^{1-i\delta}-1}{(1-i\delta)(T-1)}$.
Its modulus tends to $(1+\delta^2)^{-1/2}\neq0$, so these characters do not
decorrelate at all.
\item For $e^{-i\delta t}$ on $[1,T]$ with Haar measure the average is at
most $2/(|\delta|\log T)$ in modulus.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $x=e^\tau$; integrate directly; integrate by parts once.
\end{proof}
No integrality enters any of these statements. Moreover $\mathbb N$ is not a
subgroup of $(\R_{>0},\times)$; the subgroup it generates, $\Q_{>0}$, is
dense. The ``arithmetic decorrelation gap'' of the earlier public description
therefore has nothing to explain.

\section{Scope limits}\label{sec:limits}
The following limits were already stated correctly in v12. We retain them:
\begin{itemize}
\item A nonempty proper subset of $SU(2)$ is not invariant under the full left
action. This applies to a constrained source lift once one is specified.
\item The ratio $2\pi^2R^3/(\pi r^2)$ with $r=\phi^{-2}R$ equals $2\pi\phi^4R$.
It carries a length scale and defines no mass operator.
\item For the canonical Kähler form the two power coordinates Poisson-commute,
whereas the full $SU(2)$ moment map is a different observable algebra.
\item The circle operator $-i\partial_\theta$ with antiperiodic condition has
spectrum $\Z+\frac12$, which is symmetric, so its eta invariant vanishes
\cite{aps}.
\item The real geometry specifies no complex algebraic pair, finite-field
model or Frobenius. A Weil cohomology theory is extra input. Given such a
theory with localization and Tate twists, the pair $(E,D)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:a3} has a one-dimensional boundary term.
\end{itemize}

\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}
\begin{itemize}
\item Whether a quadratic readout realizes $S$ on $D_+$ under the historical
powers (Remark~\ref{rem:data}). Whether any principle selects a canonical
non-analytic readout; Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth} suggests that none does
without further input.
\item A sign-twisted, form or spinor operator whose boundary data are compatible
with the carriers of Proposition~\ref{prop:carrier}.
\item The actual singular Dirac and Pin problem of v12 Sections~8.20--8.24:
closed domains for the twisted Dirac operator on the actual Whitney metric,
graph and overlap estimates, singular-cut trace spaces, and
Clifford-compatible transmission maps. Exact-cone and regular-cut results
remain model or conditional statements. The scalar theory needed as a first
step is developed in \cite{companion}.
\item Independent signal or physical models for capacity, mass, clocks and
chirality. None follows from the geometry above.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item derivative determinants, the Euclidean jet invariants and the polar
remainder;

\item the edge and skew-line algebra;

None of these replays certifies an analytic, sheaf-theoretic or operator-domain
statement.

fibres of the cross-cap lift, the square-root and logarithm lifting, the Mellin
interval identity, the Gram entries and eigenvalue enclosure, the quadrature
readout identities and the cusp factorization. This is partial coverage of
selected statements, not a formalization of the paper.
